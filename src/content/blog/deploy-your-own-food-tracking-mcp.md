My friend Jordan built a diet tracker where he tells Claude what he ate and Claude writes it to a Google Sheet. I’d quit MyFitnessPal after about a year of typing in every meal, so I wanted something I could just talk to. I’d never built an MCP before, so I built one: a Cloudflare Worker that looks up foods in USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts and saves my logs to D1.

Then I turned it into a template so you can run your own copy.

**Get the template:** [github.com/redSlug/healthy-mcp-template](https://github.com/redSlug/healthy-mcp-template)

![Telling Claude on my phone that I am eating three Whoppers candies, and Claude logging them to my food tracker](https://images.bradleydettmer.me/food-tracking-mcp/01-claude-logging-whoppers.png "phone")

## What you get

- Log meals by talking, typing or sharing pictures with Claude (or your fave MCP client), in the middle of any conversation
- Nutrition data from free food databases ([USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) and [Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/))
- Your logs stored in your own Cloudflare D1 database, not someone else's app
- Free to run: it currently fits in Cloudflare's free tier, D1 included, and the food databases are free
- Code you can change: edit the migrations, add features, redeploy

## Set it up

The steps are in the [healthy-mcp-template README](https://github.com/redSlug/healthy-mcp-template).

Then say something like "I had oatmeal with blueberries for breakfast" and it's logged.

The MCP server runs on a Cloudflare Worker. Claude calls it to search foods and save logs, and the Worker stores your logs in D1.

## Things I learned

- Approximate data is fine — I spent about half a day architecting around FatSecret, which needs a dedicated IP address and only lets you cache its data for 24 hours. Then I realized [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) and [Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/) combined with AI were plenty, and better for running reports across many days.
- MCP clients send JSON but expect text back — that surprised me, since this was my first MCP.
